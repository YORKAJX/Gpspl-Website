Consultant Design Notes

How GPSPL evaluates a conference-room AV requirement.

A professional meeting-room BOQ should explain the logic behind display, camera, microphone, sound, control, installation and support scope. These notes help purchase teams and facility teams understand why a room may need a simple video bar, a DSP-based audio system, a larger display, a second screen or a controlled source-switching workflow.

Viewing-distance and display planning

Display size is checked against the farthest regular viewer, the type of content and the room layout. A small room used mostly for video calls may need one commercial display or interactive display. A deeper boardroom may need a larger 86 inch, 98 inch or dual-display setup so remote participants and shared content remain readable. For training rooms, the screen should support spreadsheet, drawing, presentation and whiteboard visibility from the last row. GPSPL also checks wall surface, mounting height, cable access, service clearance, glare, natural light and future display replacement before freezing the exact model.

Camera coverage and participant framing

Camera recommendation depends on seating depth, table width, audience orientation and meeting workflow. A short huddle room can often use a video bar with integrated audio. A boardroom with long-table seating may require PTZ framing, speaker tracking or preset recall. Training rooms often need presenter framing plus audience visibility. GPSPL avoids adding camera quantity only because the room has more seats; the practical question is whether the selected camera can capture the important participants at a natural angle without making people look too small or distorted.

Microphone pickup and acoustic risk

Voice pickup is selected from table, ceiling, boundary, wireless or beamforming options after looking at table shape, ceiling type, height, background noise and how people participate. A room with a fixed boardroom table may suit table microphones or beamforming ceiling microphones. A flexible room may need wireless handheld or lapel microphones. A glass-heavy room, exposed ceiling, hard flooring or noisy HVAC condition may need a DSP, better echo cancellation and acoustic review. The objective is clear speech capture with the minimum practical number of microphones, not a long equipment list.

Speaker coverage, DSP and amplifier headroom

Room sound is planned for speech intelligibility, even coverage and reliable conferencing. Speaker selection considers listener distance, ceiling height, coverage angle, speaker sensitivity and usable headroom. Ceiling speakers are useful for distributed speech coverage, while wall or column speakers may suit rooms with special ceiling conditions. DSP is recommended when echo cancellation, microphone mixing, audio routing, gain control or future expansion is required. Amplifier capacity is selected with headroom so the system does not sound strained, but GPSPL avoids over-sizing when a lower-power amplifier can meet the room requirement cleanly.

Control simplicity and supportability

A premium meeting room should still feel simple to the user. Touch controls, source switching, room scheduler panels, presets and automation are added only when they reduce real user friction. For example, a leadership boardroom may need one-touch meeting start, display power, source selection, camera presets and volume control. A small BYOD room may only need reliable cable access and clear instructions. GPSPL also considers IT support handover: device IPs, cable labels, rack drawings, control logic, training and warranty documentation should be easy to understand after installation.

Installation, commissioning and lifecycle readiness

Installation scope is not only mounting devices. A professional room includes cable laying, conduit coordination, rack assembly, power and network readiness, display alignment, camera mounting, microphone placement, speaker installation, DSP tuning, video calibration, testing, user training and documentation. The final estimate also depends on access height, ceiling type, wall readiness, cable route length and whether work must happen after office hours. GPSPL keeps this visible so clients understand that a working meeting room is delivered through engineering effort, not only hardware purchase.

Buyer Guide

Conference room AV setup for boardrooms, meeting rooms, huddle spaces and training rooms.

Organizations searching for a conference room AV setup in Delhi NCR or India usually need more than a camera and a display. The room has to support daily calls, client presentations, leadership reviews, hybrid training, content sharing and reliable handover to IT or facility teams. GPSPL approaches each room as a complete working environment. The display must be readable, the microphone pickup must be clear, the speaker coverage must be comfortable, the camera must frame the right people, the source-sharing workflow must be predictable and the cabling must remain serviceable after handover.

For huddle rooms, the best design may be a compact display, video bar, simple BYOD input and clean power/cable access. For meeting rooms, GPSPL may add a better commercial display, wireless presentation, stronger camera coverage and room-ready audio. For boardrooms, the design often becomes more integrated: PTZ camera, ceiling or table microphones, DSP, amplifier, speakers, controller, touch panel, rack, UPS and documented commissioning. For training and classroom rooms, audience visibility, instructor mobility, projection distance, microphone style, recording needs and display brightness become more important.

GPSPL can also help clients avoid two common mistakes. The first is under-designing a room so users complain about poor audio, small text, weak camera framing or confusing connections. The second is over-designing a room with unnecessary premium products that increase cost without improving the actual user experience. A good BOQ sits between these two problems. It gives the client professional performance, serviceable installation, future scalability and a cost direction that can be validated after site survey.

Site Survey Priorities

Why final conference-room pricing is validated after site review.

A planning estimate can guide the buyer, but a final conference-room AV quotation should be checked against the real site. GPSPL reviews wall strength, cable entry points, floor boxes, conduit availability, ceiling tile type, slab height, projector throw, display mounting surface, network rack distance, power socket availability, UPS location and access for future service. These details affect installation effort, cable length, mounting accessories, rack layout and the final model family.

Acoustic condition is equally important. Glass partitions, open ceilings, hard flooring, exposed HVAC noise and long reflective tables can change microphone and DSP requirements. In a quiet compact room, a simple room audio device may be sufficient. In a boardroom with more reflections, better microphone placement and DSP tuning may be needed. In a training room, presenter movement and audience questions may require a different microphone strategy.

This is why GPSPL shows a professional planning range first and confirms final commercials after site validation, brand selection and client approval. The client gets a clear starting direction without being misled into believing that every room can be priced accurately from one screenshot or one seat-count number.

Buyer Questions

Questions clients usually ask before finalizing a meeting room.

How do you decide display size?

Display size depends on viewing distance, seating depth, content type, wall condition and whether the room is used for presentations, video calls or training.

Do all rooms need DSP and ceiling microphones?

No. Small rooms may work with a video bar or table audio. DSP and ceiling microphones are recommended when coverage, echo cancellation, scalability or room quality needs justify them.

Can GPSPL quote only selected items?

Yes. The scope can be display-only, audio-only, video conferencing-only, LED wall-only or a complete integrated meeting room with installation and support.

Why is site survey still needed?

Final model, mounting, cable route, network readiness, power backup and acoustic risk depend on actual site conditions, ceiling, table layout and wall readiness.
