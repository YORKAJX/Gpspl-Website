LED Video Wall vs Projector: Which is Better for Your Office?

LED walls and projectors both display large-format content — but they are fundamentally different technologies built for different environments. This guide breaks down every factor so you can decide confidently.

The Core Difference

A projector is a light-throwing device — it shines an image onto a reflective screen. An LED video wall is a self-emitting display — each pixel produces its own light directly. This single difference drives every other trade-off: brightness, ambient light performance, installation complexity, maintenance cost, and lifespan.

Neither technology is universally better. The right choice depends entirely on your specific environment, budget, and use case.

Brightness & Ambient Light

This is where LED walls have a decisive advantage. LED walls produce 500–1,500 nits of brightness or more. Projectors produce the equivalent of 100–400 nits on screen depending on the throw ratio and gain screen used.

In a room with significant natural light or overhead lighting, a projector image washes out quickly. LED walls remain vivid and readable in bright environments — making them the correct choice for lobbies, boardrooms with glass walls, or open-plan spaces.

Rule of Thumb If your room has windows that cannot be fully blinded during presentations, choose an LED wall. If you have full light control, a high-lumen projector is a cost-effective alternative.

When to Choose an LED Video Wall

  • Your space has significant ambient light (glass-fronted boardrooms, lobbies)
  • You need 24x7 display (digital signage, reception screens)
  • Image size required is larger than 120" reliably
  • You want a premium appearance — thin, seamless, no screen required
  • Long-term ownership cost is a priority

When to Choose a Projector

  • Budget is the primary constraint — projectors cost significantly less upfront
  • The room has full light control (blackout blinds, no windows)
  • You need a large image occasionally, not continuously
  • The space is a training hall, classroom, or auditorium with controlled lighting
  • Portability is needed — projectors can be moved between rooms

Understanding LED Pixel Pitch

For LED walls, pixel pitch determines image sharpness at a given viewing distance. Smaller pixel pitch = sharper image but higher cost.

Pixel PitchMin. Viewing DistanceTypical Use
P1.5 – P2.01.5 – 2.5 metresSmall boardrooms, control rooms
P2.5 – P3.02.5 – 4 metresMedium conference rooms, showrooms
P4.0 – P6.04 – 8 metresLarge halls, event spaces
P8.0+8+ metresOutdoor, large venues

Our Verdict

For most corporate conference rooms and boardrooms in 2026, if the budget allows, we recommend an LED video wall — the long-term ownership cost is often comparable to a projector when you factor in lamp replacements and service calls over 5–7 years, and the image quality and reliability are superior.

For training centres, classrooms, and large halls with controlled lighting, a high-quality laser projector (which eliminates lamp replacement) remains an excellent, cost-effective choice.
