Conference Room AV Setup Guide 2026 — Equipment, Cost & BOQ

A complete guide for setting up a professional AV system in your conference room — from display selection and audio to control systems, cabling, and real BOQ estimates for Delhi NCR projects.

Why a Proper AV Setup Matters for Conference Rooms

A conference room is one of the highest-use spaces in any office. Poor audio — muffled speech, echo, dropped calls — costs real productivity. A dim or incorrectly sized display makes presentations unreadable from the back row. A missing HDMI cable at the wrong moment delays a client meeting.

Done right, a well-designed conference room AV system becomes invisible — participants focus on the meeting, not the technology. This guide walks you through every component decision, from room size to display type to audio processing, with actual cost ranges based on Delhi NCR project data from GPSPL.

Pro Tip Always design AV around the room's longest throw distance and the seat farthest from the display. If that person cannot read text clearly, the system has failed — regardless of how expensive it is.

Step 1 — Choosing the Right Display

Display selection is the most visible decision in any conference room AV project. The right choice depends on three variables: room size, ambient light, and content type.

Interactive Flat Panels (IFP) — LG CreateBoard, Samsung Flip, or ViewSonic — are popular for medium rooms where annotation and whiteboarding is needed. They function as both a display and a collaboration tool.

Step 2 — Audio System Selection

Audio quality in a conference room depends on three elements: microphone pickup, echo cancellation (AEC), and speaker coverage. Modern unified communication systems bundle these together.

  • Ceiling microphone arrays (Shure MXA910, Biamp Devio) — best for large rooms, no table clutter
  • Tabletop conferencing units (Jabra PanaCast, Poly Studio) — plug-and-play, ideal for small rooms
  • Dante-networked audio (QSC, BSS) — for multi-zone rooms requiring routing flexibility
  • In-ceiling speakers — provide even coverage for all seats, used with DSP amplifiers
Common Mistake Using consumer-grade Bluetooth speakers in a conference room. These have poor AEC, high latency, and break down under heavy use. Always specify commercial-grade conferencing audio.

Step 3 — Unified Communication (UC) Platform

Most conference rooms today must support Microsoft Teams, Zoom, Google Meet, and sometimes Cisco Webex. The AV system must be certified or compatible with the primary platform your organisation uses.

Options include:

  • Microsoft Teams Rooms (MTR) — Lenovo ThinkSmart, Poly, Logitech Rally certified rooms
  • Zoom Rooms — dedicated hardware kits with room controllers
  • Bring-Your-Own-Device (BYOD) — HDMI + USB connectivity allowing any laptop to join any platform

Step 4 — Control System

Larger conference rooms benefit from a centralised control system (Crestron, AMX, or Control4) that allows one-touch operation: "Start Meeting" turns on the display, lowers the blinds, starts the camera, and dials into Teams — automatically.

Smaller rooms can use simpler touch panels or even smartphone control apps. GPSPL designs and programs both simple and complex control scenarios across Delhi NCR projects.

Step 5 — Cabling Infrastructure

Cabling is invisible but critical. Poor cabling causes 80% of AV failures. Key requirements:

  • HDMI 2.0 / 2.1 — for 4K content, maximum 10m active cable recommended
  • HDBaseT extenders — for runs beyond 10m over Cat6
  • USB-C hub at the table — one cable connection for laptop power + video + audio
  • Structured cabling — Cat6A for all network-connected AV devices
  • Conduit planning — all cables must be in conduit for maintenance access
