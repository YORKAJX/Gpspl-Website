What should a broadcast studio AV brief include?

Start with the type of programme, number of presenters, camera positions and whether output is recorded, streamed or transmitted. Include your editing, graphics, newsroom automation and playout systems so interfaces and licences can be checked against the chosen workflow.

For a running newsroom, list equipment to reuse and the permitted changeover window. A new studio needs a room plan, lighting positions, acoustic requirements, network information and power arrangements.

Displays, monitoring and control rooms

Separate presenter backdrops from operator multiview displays. Provide the number of video sources, desired layouts, viewing distances and whether the content must appear correctly on camera. Ask for demonstrations of the shortlisted display and camera combination before approving an LED backdrop.

Explore Active LED displays, control-room AV and KVM and signal switching.

Camera, microphone and lighting integration

Share camera models, microphone types, mixer inputs and how operators control the system. Specify the required connections and cable distances. For lighting, identify presenter locations and available rigging points. The final design should state who supplies mounts, cabling, network configuration and electrical work.

Can I buy equipment for an existing studio?

Yes. Send the model, quantity, existing connections and delivery city for a supply quotation. For a complete project, request a room-wise BOQ with equipment, integration, testing, training and handover responsibilities.

How is studio handover checked?

Agree a test plan before installation. It should cover each source and destination, speech playback, camera presets, lighting scenes, operator access and the required recording or streaming workflow. Confirm ongoing support, warranty and recovery procedures in the quotation.
